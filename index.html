Control logic
GREENZone empty – normal crane speed.
AMBERPerson inside zone but outside hook danger radius – horn pulse, crane at creep speed.
REDPerson inside danger radius – safe stop (STO/SS1), siren, strobe. Stays red until zone verified empty for 3 s and manual reset.

System architecture

1. Sensing4–6 perimeter 3D LiDAR (or stereo 3D) at 6–8 m, overlapping views; 2–4 safety scanners / safety 3D on bridge & trolley.
2. Edge computePerson detection, tracking, virtual-fence crossing, occupancy count, video evidence. Non-safety rated.
3. Safety PLCDual-channel safety logic, zone states, reset, muting rules. Rated PL d / Cat 3 minimum (per risk assessment).
4. Crane drivesSTO / SS1 on hoist, trolley, bridge; brake monitoring.
5. AlertsGreen/amber/red tower lights, horn, strobe, operator panel, floor-level flashers.

Sensor placement

• Perimeter sensors on columns/trusses, 6–8 m high, tilted 30–45° inward; every floor point seen by at least two sensors.
• Bridge/trolley sensors look straight down so the area beneath the hook is never hidden by racks or the load.
• Safety-rated scanners guard the hook danger zone; AI/LiDAR layer provides count, zone entry/exit and early amber warning.
• Keep all sensors’ detection and the zone boundary larger than the crane stopping distance (ISO 13855 logic).

Verify “area clear”

All sensors show zero occupancy for a set time (e.g. 3–5 s) → no latched/unconfirmed tracks → a person at a position with full view of the zone presses a manual reset → crane may resume. Never auto-resume from the count alone.

Your 10 questions

1. Best technology?

A combination. Use 3D LiDAR (or 3D stereo vision) for detecting, tracking and counting; AI cameras optionally for person-vs-vehicle classification and video evidence; safety-rated scanners / safety 3D vision (IEC 61496) for the actual stop function. Radar is useful if dust or steam affects optics. Do not rely on AI cameras alone for the safety function.

2. Can it count in an open area from any direction?

Yes, with a virtual boundary (fence) and multi-sensor tracking. Typical accuracy is high in good conditions but not perfect – counts drift with crowding and occlusion. So the safety decision should use zone occupancy (anyone present), and the count is information, re-zeroed when the zone is verified empty.

3. Entering, leaving, turning around?

Each person gets a track ID. Entry/exit is registered only when a track crosses the boundary and stays beyond a hysteresis band (about 0.3–0.5 m) for a short time. Turning back inside the band does not count; movement inside the zone is just tracked.

4. How many sensors, where?

Rough starting point for a ~30 × 15 m bay: 4–6 perimeter sensors + 2–4 on the crane. Final number comes from a site survey and field-of-view simulation, considering racks, machines and mounting heights.

5. Groups or people close together?

3D point-cloud tracking separates people about 0.3–0.5 m apart. Tight clusters may be under-counted, but presence is still detected, so the safety response is unaffected.

6. Hidden behind equipment?

Overlapping views from different angles and top-down crane sensors reduce blind spots. Add a “last-seen-inside” latch: if a person disappears inside the zone, they stay counted until seen leaving or cleared by a sweep and reset. Layout rule: no floor point covered by only one sensor.

7. Integration with safety PLC / crane control?

Yes. Safety-rated sensors connect by OSSD or safety fieldbus (PROFIsafe / CIP Safety) to a safety PLC (e.g. Siemens S7-1500F, Rockwell GuardLogix, Pilz). Edge-computer count data goes over standard Ethernet as non-safety information.

8. Automatic safe stop?

Yes, if the crane drives have safe-torque-off / safe-stop inputs. A retrofit must be reviewed with the crane OEM or a qualified integrator, and zone sizes must include the measured stopping distance.

10. Standards & performance levels

ISO 12100 (risk assessment); ISO 13849-1/-2 (target PL d, Cat 3 – risk assessment decides, possibly higher); IEC 62061 / IEC 61508 (SIL 2 equivalent); IEC 61496-1/-3/-4-3 (sensors); ISO 13855 (safety distances); ISO 13850 (e-stop); IEC 60204-32 (crane electrical); plus national crane and OHS rules (e.g. Bangladesh Labour Act / local code, ISO 4301). Safety functions must be validated, not just installed.
